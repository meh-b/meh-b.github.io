The Bose gas

The Lee–Huang–Yang formula for the hard-core Simple Equation

M. E. H. Bahri and I. Jauslin

Manuscript (2026). To be submitted to Commun. Math. Phys.

We prove that the energy given by the Simple Equation of Lieb’s Simplified Approach for the hard-core potential satisfies the Lee–Huang–Yang formula at low density. The results extend beyond the energy to the condensate fraction and the momentum distribution.

Lee–Yang zeros

Lee–Yang zeros and particle fluctuations

M. E. H. Bahri, I. Jauslin and J. L. Lebowitz

arXiv:2607.26975 (2026). Under review at J. Stat. Phys.

For classical particles in the continuum with a stable, tempered and lower-regular pair potential, we prove that if the Lee–Yang zeros of the grand canonical partition function stay a positive distance away from a point z0 > 0 for all sufficiently large volumes, then the thermodynamic limit along cubes commutes with differentiation with respect to the chemical potential at z0. In particular, the density and the particle-number variance per unit volume converge to β−1∂p/∂μ and β−2∂2p/∂μ2 respectively, independently of the boundary condition.

A new perspective on thermally fluctuating 2D elastic membranes: introducing odd elastic moduli and non-equilibrium effects

M. E. H. Bahri, S. Sarkar, D. A. Matoz-Fernandez and A. Košmrlj

arXiv:2307.05749 (2023). Under review at Phys. Rev. Lett.

We study thermally fluctuating two-dimensional elastic membranes with odd elastic moduli, whose non-equilibrium behavior cannot be described by a Hamiltonian and is treated with Langevin equations. A renormalization group analysis shows that one odd modulus becomes negligible at long length scales while the other persists. We also introduce a simulation method in which the discrete and continuum in-plane elastic moduli correspond directly, so that no coarse-graining is needed.

Statistical mechanics of nanotubes

S. Sarkar, M. E. H. Bahri and A. Košmrlj

arXiv:2305.14602 (2023). Under review at Phys. Rev. B.

In flat two-dimensional sheets, thermal fluctuations renormalize the elastic moduli beyond a temperature-dependent length scale. In nanotubes, the competition between stretching and bending caused by radial fluctuations introduces a further elastic length scale, set by the tube radius and effective thickness. Beyond it, the axial in-plane modulus and the bending rigidity in the circumferential direction stop renormalizing, while the circumferential in-plane modulus keeps renormalizing with different universal exponents. We verify these results with molecular dynamics simulations.

Mechanical properties of fluctuating elastic membranes under uni-axial tension

M. E. H. Bahri, S. Sarkar and A. Košmrlj

arXiv:2209.09350 (2022). Under review at Phys. Rev. B.

We study the response of isotropic atomically thin sheets, such as graphene, to uniaxial tension, combining a self-consistent screening analysis with molecular dynamics simulations. Beyond a stress-dependent length scale the effective in-plane elastic constants become strongly anisotropic, with different scaling along and perpendicular to the stress, while the bending rigidities show no comparable anomaly. At moderate tension the stress–strain relation is nonlinear and universal, and at large tension the Young’s modulus of the bare material is recovered.

Statistical mechanics of freely fluctuating two-dimensional elastic crystals

M. E. H. Bahri and Ya. G. Sinai

J. Stat. Phys. 180 (2020), 739–748, doi:10.1007/s10955-020-02512-4. Special issue in honor of Joel L. Lebowitz.

This note reviews the statistical mechanics of freely fluctuating two-dimensional crystals with a nonlinear elastic Hamiltonian. It discusses their existence in view of the Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem, the crumpling transition of freely suspended crystalline membranes, and the renormalization of elastic moduli.
